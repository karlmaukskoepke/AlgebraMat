# Flip it: solve equations with an opposite box by flipping everything  (Karl, 2026-10-12; revision 1.0; status: designed)

**Follows `SPEC-SOLVE-IT-LOOP.md` rev 1.1** (including its conformance checklist). Everything shared (the screen, the guess-or-move choice at every state, the forced-move rule, substitution checks, Undo and Start over, the work stack and folding, the help row, scaffolds, generator rules) is defined there and wins any conflict. This spec states only what is specific to Flip it.

Section: Solve it.   Prerequisite card(s): **Divide it** (L4 uses Take a fraction of both sides). Old levels: old One-step `−x = b` maps here (Claude Code must read the real old definitions; see §8).

## 1. The task

**Move trained:** **Flip everything** (every sign on both sides changes; annotation "take the opposite of both sides"). From L4, the card also uses **Take a fraction of both sides**, which students already know from Divide it.

**Example:** `−x = 12`. The student chooses **Use a move** and taps **Flip everything**. One tap, no amount. The work stack gets `−(−x) = −(12)`, the two minus signs on x's side cancel on their own, and the next row is `x = −(12)`. The right side is **not** simplified, so the student works out **−12** and types it into the `x = [▁]` row. The check follows (loop §4): `−(−12) = 12`, `12 = 12 ✓ Balanced`.

**Two-move example:** `−3x = 12`. Both orders are legal and offered:
- **Flip first:** `−(−3x) = −(12)` → `3x = −12` (a partial step is evaluated). The choice returns. Then **Take ⅓**: `⅓(3x) = ⅓(−12)` → `x = ⅓(−12)`. The student types **−4**.
- **Fraction first:** `⅓(−3x) = ⅓(12)` → `−x = 4`. The choice returns. Then **Flip**: `−(−x) = −(4)` → `x = −(4)`. The student types **−4**.

**What they type:** one whole number on the integer pad, into the `x = [▁]` row in the work stack. **The solved value is never displayed** before the student types it.

**Picture (shared by the numeric level and the scaffolds):** counters turn into their opposites (+ becomes −, − becomes +) and **boxes turn into opposite boxes** (a regular box becomes an opposite box and back). An opposite box is drawn as an opposite box, never as "a box with a minus". A flip changes every counter and every box on both sides at once.

**Words:** box, **opposite box**, counters, border, add the opposite, balance, check. **"Flip" is reserved for signs.** "Trade places" (swapping sides) is a different move on its own card. We never say "multiply by −1" on the button; the move is **Flip everything**.

## 2. Levels (6, fixed; L1 is numeric)

Five problems per level, **no repeated answers within a level**. All problems are generated by code and verified by substituting the answer (loop §8). **Size is set by the counter model**: at most **36 counters per side**, at most 6 boxes. The numbers are not operated on much here, so the ranges stay gentle and progress comes from the move.

| Level | Forms | Numbers | What's new |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Numeric: `a + b = c + d`, and items with subtraction | results at most 15 in size, no side equal to 0 | The balance under a flip: flipping both sides keeps it; flipping one side upsets it (§3D). |
| **L2** | `−x = b` | answers 2-12 in size (b = −answer) | Flip everything, one tap. The opposite box becomes a regular box. |
| **L3** | `b = −x` | answers 2-12 in size | x on the right. Rewriting `b = −x` as `−x = b` is not taught; the student just flips. |
| **L4** | `−ax = b` | a = 2-6, a divides b, at least 3 different a, answers 2-9 in size and never ±a, \|b\| ≤ 36 | Flip and take a fraction, in either order. |
| **L5** | Mixed: `−x = b`, `b = −x`, `−ax = b`, `b = −ax` | as L2-L4 | x on the right with a coefficient. |
| **L6** | Mixed, as L5 | **bigger answers**, \|b\| ≤ 36 | Bigger numbers. At least 3 of the 5 have an answer of 8 or more in size. |

**Form counts per level (required mix of the 5 problems)**
- **L1:** 2 "do" items, 3 "quiz" items, then one watched walkthrough (§3D).
- **L2:** at least 2 with a positive b (negative answers) and 2 with a negative b (positive answers).
- **L3:** same sign mix as L2.
- **L4:** at least 3 different coefficients; at least 2 positive b and 2 negative b.
- **L5:** at least 1 of each of the four forms; at least 2 with x on the right; at least 2 positive b and 2 negative b.
- **L6:** same as L5.

**Do NOT make every answer positive or every answer negative.** A negative b gives a positive answer and the double negative is where students slip.

**Sample banks (answers checked by substitution; the generator must do the same automatically)**
- L2: `−x=5` → −5 · `−x=−8` → 8 · `−x=12` → −12 · `−x=−3` → 3 · `−x=9` → −9
- L3: `7=−x` → −7 · `−4=−x` → 4 · `10=−x` → −10 · `−9=−x` → 9 · `2=−x` → −2
- L4: `−3x=12` → −4 · `−2x=−14` → 7 · `−4x=20` → −5 · `−5x=−15` → 3 · `−6x=18` → −3
- L5: `15=−3x` → −5 · `−8=−2x` → 4 · `−x=6` → −6 · `20=−5x` → −4 · `−7=−x` → 7
- L6: `−4x=32` → −8 · `−3x=−33` → 11 · `36=−3x` → −12 · `−x=17` → −17 · `−2x=−30` → 15

## 3. The model / picture

### 3A. Moves on this card (the shared list, loop §5)
- **Flip everything:** active from L2. **Take a fraction of both sides:** active from L4 (the menu always shows all five fractions, as on Divide it, and wrong ones fail visibly).
- **Every other move is greyed** (faint Why? outlines). Tapping a greyed move says in the line: for a move from an **earlier card** (Add to both sides), "You don't need that move here."; for a move from a **later card**, "That move unlocks on a later card." On **L2-L3, Take a fraction is greyed** with "You don't need that move here." (it goes live at L4).
- On **L1**, only Flip everything is active.

### 3B. Flip everything: one action
1. In move mode, **tap Flip everything.** It takes **no amount**, so selecting it **is** the action: one tap, no Go, no blinking cursor. (On touch devices the icon first shows its text for a moment, loop §5; Undo covers an accidental tap.)
2. **One action:** the work stack gets a row with the flip on **both** sides at once, the flip sign **in colour on both sides** (`−(−x) = −(12)`).
3. **The cancelling is shown.** The next row shows the double negative on x's side visibly cancelling (struck through, then dropped). A flip that turns a regular box opposite is shown the same way. **The other side is left as the flipped expression** (`x = −(12)`, `x = −(−7)`) so the answer isn't shown, **unless the step is not final**: on `−3x = 12`, flipping first gives `3x = −12` (a partial step is evaluated).
4. **Why? boxes are judged** after the move (§3E). When x is alone with a positive coefficient, the line says "x is alone. What is it? Type it.", **Solved for x** turns green, and the `x = [▁]` row goes live in the stack with the blinking cursor. The student works the number out and types it. **Check ✓** and the substitution follow the loop (§4 there).
5. **A flip that makes things worse** (flipping a state that is already positive, e.g., `3x = −12` → `−3x = 12`): **No opposite box** turns **red**, the line says "That made the box opposite. We want a regular box.", and **Undo** is highlighted. Illegal moves do not exist.

### 3C. Take a fraction (L4 and on)
- It works as on Divide it: pick one of `× ½, × ⅓, × ¼, × ⅕, × ⅙` (with the small `÷ n` line), then Go. Both sides update at once and the record is `⅓(−3x) = ⅓(12)` → `−x = 4`. A fraction that doesn't split evenly **fails visibly** and is undone. The menu is never narrowed.
- **Fraction first is legal** and is not punished (§3E). The state `−x = 4` offers the choice again.
- **Dealing counters into groups is a scaffold, never the bare default,** and **the dealing scaffold never deals into an opposite box** (see §4).
- *How a fraction is actually dealt and kept is defined by the Divide it light-build pass; this card reuses it.* (Open, §8.)

### 3D. Level 1: the numeric level (the only level where the balance shows by default)
The screen shows numbers only, standing on a seesaw as groups of counters, with a big readout: **`5 = 5   Balanced ✓`**. The prompt is in the line. **Every item ends with a green Next.** Nothing advances on a timer and the layout never shifts. Five items, then one walkthrough:
1. **Do, flip both:** `4 + 1 = 2 + 3`. The student taps **Flip everything**. Two highlighted buttons: **Flip left** (`−5 = 5   Unbalanced ✗`, the counters and boxes on the left turn opposite, the beam tips), then **Flip right** (`−5 = −5   Balanced ✓`, the beam levels). Then Go.
2. **Do, with subtraction:** `5 − 2 = 1 + 2` (`3 = 3`). Flip left gives `−5 + 2 = 1 + 2` (`−3 = 3 ✗`); flip right gives `−5 + 2 = −1 − 2` (`−3 = −3 ✓`). **Every sign flips, including the one on the subtraction.** Two actions are the point of the level.
3. **Quiz:** "What happens if we **FLIP THE LEFT SIDE ONLY**? Does it keep the balance, or upset it?" (*Keeps the balance* / *Upsets the balance*.)
4. **Quiz:** "What happens if we **FLIP BOTH SIDES**?"
5. **Quiz:** "What happens if we **FLIP THE RIGHT SIDE ONLY**?"
- **After either answer, right or wrong, the work stack shows what happened,** one line each, ending in a Balanced or Unbalanced mark. For item 3: `4 + 1 = 2 + 3`, `−(4 + 1) = 2 + 3`, `−5 = 5`, `✗ Unbalanced`. The line says whether the guess was right ("Yes!" or "You said keeps. Look: it upsets the balance."), then the green Next. **No retry.** A wrong answer gets the evidence and Next.
6. **Final walkthrough (a watched demonstration, no typing; nothing is erased).** `−x = 3` on the bare screen with a **balance indicator at the top** and the note **"We don't know x yet, but the = sign tells us both sides are balanced."** Each animation plays, then waits for a click on the one highlighted target: (a) highlighted **Flip everything**; (b) the stack adds `−(−x) = −(3)`, the flip in colour on both sides; (c) **Next:** the double negative cancels (struck through) and the stack adds `x = −(3)`, with the Why? boxes going green (**No opposite box**, **Solved for x**); (d) **Next:** the stack adds `x = −3`, line **"Now we have x = −3."** (the **only place the value is shown for the student**); (e) **Next:** under the stack, **"Let's plug x = −3 back in:"** `−(−3) = 3`, `✓ Balanced`. Takeaway in the line: **"Flipping everything turns an opposite box into a regular box."** Then a green Next moves to L2. No sixth dot (the five dots, then the label "Walkthrough"). The Move/Why? panel is visible with **only Flip everything active**.
- Sides are never 0, so flipping one side can never balance by accident (the generator checks). Quiz answers are shuffled. Numbers vary each time. **L1 replay is the same L1.**
- **"I'm stuck" and "Teach me" are not offered on this level** (loop §6).
- **No stray element from L1 survives into L2.**

### 3E. The Why? boxes (green ✓ / red ✗ / grey –), judged only after the move
| Box | Beside | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **No opposite box** | Flip everything | **Green** if no term of x has a negative coefficient after the move. **Red** if the move made the coefficient of x negative. **Grey** if it didn't change. |
| **Smaller numbers** (L4 on) | Take a fraction | **Green** when every number in the equation gets smaller in size. **Grey** if unchanged. Never red. |
| **One box** (L4 on) | Take a fraction | **Green** when there is a single box on x's side (coefficient of size 1, regular or opposite). |
| **Solved for x** | bottom of the panel (uber-goal) | **Green** when x is alone on its side **with a positive coefficient** (this is exactly when No opposite box and One box are both green). |

- **The lights are an unranked checklist** (loop §5). Both orders are legal and neither is punished: after a fraction-first move on `−3x = 12`, **Smaller numbers** and **One box** turn green and **No opposite box** stays grey (not red, because the move was legal); the flip then turns it green and **Solved for x** goes green.
- **Smaller numbers** is a progress light, not a gate: on `−x = 12` the numbers can't get smaller, and Solved for x does not wait for it.
- L2-L3 show only **No opposite box** and **Solved for x** (Take a fraction's boxes are faint outlines).
- **Solved for x** is redefined here (positive coefficient). The pilot's definition (x alone) is unaffected, because the pilot never has a negative coefficient.

## 4. Supports (the ladder)

All supports follow the loop (§6). Specific to this card:

### "I'm stuck" (one rung per click; each click = +1 stuck count; rungs restart at each new equation state)
- **Rung 1: Model it.** The seesaw appears **pre-built** with the equation standing on it (opposite boxes drawn as opposite boxes, the other side a pile of counters), the balance word on. The opposite box is highlighted: "This box is opposite. What would make it a regular box?" It ends with "Do you know x now?" and the pad. **Pre-built at every level of this card** (the new idea is the flip, not building the picture).
- **Rung 2: Choose the move.** Flip everything opens in its **two-action form**: the student flips the left side (the box turns regular, the beam tips, "Unbalanced"), then flips the right side (the counters turn to their opposites, the beam levels). Ends with the pad.
- **Rung 3: Guided.** The flip is pre-selected and only the next target is highlighted. On L4 and up, once the flip is done, the next needed move is **Take a fraction**: its button is highlighted and the student deals and keeps one group.
- **After every scaffold the choice returns:** guess, type the value, or try a move. I'm stuck is always there for the next rung.
- **On a state reached by taking the fraction first** (`−x = 4`): the scaffold redraws the opposite box against the pile, and Rung 2 flips it. **The dealing scaffold never deals into an opposite box;** it always flips the boxes first.
- **x on the right** (`12 = −x`): the highlight lands on the opposite box on the right side. Nothing else changes.

### "Teach me step-by-step"
The same scaffolds on a **forced path: flip first** (so students meet positive groups before sharing), then, on L4 and up, the fraction. **Only the correct move is accepted**, and the student performs every action and types the answer. This is the "guided" order; free play accepts either order.

**L1 gets neither button.**

## 5. What a wrong answer looks like (the slips)

Typed-value slips (x alone after a move). Each wrong answer is **tagged** by recognizing which mistake produces that exact number; examples are checked by code. Messages appear in the line, after the full substitution into the original equation (loop §1B).

| Slip | Example → wrong answer | Message the student sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Kept the number's sign (dropped the flip; double negative)** | `−x=12` → **12**; `12=−x` → **12**; `−x=−7` → **−7**; `−3x=12` → **12** | If b is positive: "The flip changed the 12 too. Put 12 back in: −(12) is −12, not 12." If b is negative: "The opposite of −7 is +7. Put −7 back in and see." (The message is chosen by the sign of b. This merges what would otherwise be two slips with the same wrong answer.) |
| **Flipped, forgot to share** (a ≥ 2) | `−3x=12` → **−12** | "−12 is what's in 3 boxes. Share it: what's in one?" |
| **Shared, forgot to flip** (a ≥ 2) | `−3x=12` → **4** | "−x = 4 means the box is still opposite. Flip it." |
| **Gave the number in front** (a ≥ 2) | `−3x=12` → **3** or **−3** | "3 is how many boxes. What's in each one?" |
| **Anything else** | | a = 1: "Flip everything." a ≥ 2: "Flip everything, then share." |

**Guess path (the one named exception, loop §1B step 4).** A wrong guess substitutes into the **current** equation, ends red, and the student presses Undo. If the guess equals the **unflipped value** (`12` for `−x=12` or `12=−x`; `−7` for `−x=−7`; `12` or `4` for `−3x=12`), the line after Undo says: **"The box is opposite. When it flips, everything on the other side flips too."** and **Flip everything is highlighted**. **No scaffold opens** (message plus highlighted Flip). All other wrong guesses follow the normal path ("Let's make the equation simpler first…" with the next needed move highlighted) and get no message. A wrong guess is not a stuck click.

Move slips (judged by the Why? boxes):

| Slip | Example | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Flips a state that is already positive** | `3x = −12`, Flip → `−3x = 12` | **No opposite box red.** Line: "That made the box opposite. We want a regular box." **Undo** highlighted. |
| **Takes a fraction first** | `−3x=12`, `× ⅓` → `−x = 4` | **Legal.** Smaller numbers and One box green; No opposite box stays grey; the choice returns. |
| **Fraction doesn't split evenly** | `−3x=12`, `× ½` | Fails visibly (3 boxes can't make 2 equal groups). Undo offered; the picture names the side that broke. |
| **Adds or multiplies instead** | | Those moves are greyed on this card (§3A). |

**Wrong-move handling follows the loop:** every red or grey move gets a line and a highlighted **Undo**; three red in a row adds "Try I'm stuck."; **Start over is always live** (greyed only on the untouched original).

## 7. Edge cases and limits

- **Answers are never 0 and never ±1; for a ≥ 2, never ±a.** This keeps "gave the number in front" from colliding with the correct answer and keeps every slip's wrong answer distinct (the flipped-but-unshared `−b`, the unflipped-but-shared `b/a`, the unflipped `b`, and ±a). The generator confirms no slip's wrong answer equals the correct answer and **reports** any problem where two slips share a wrong answer.
- **a always divides b** (both orders land on whole numbers). \|b\| ≤ 36; at most 36 counters per side and 6 boxes in any scaffold; no divisor above 6 in the menu.
- **No repeated answers or equations within a level.**
- **Sides in L1 are never 0.** No item balances by accident; quiz answers are shuffled.
- **No screen ever shows the solved value** before the student types it, except the L1 walkthrough (§3D item 6).
- **Substitution check:** this card's checks are short, so they **never collapse** (full, line by line, at every level). **Worst-case work-stack height** is a fold, a two-row move, the typing row, the typed value, and a 2-line check: roughly **8 rows**. This is an estimate; the generator reports the real worst case and Claude Code renders it per loop §3B.
- **A flip on an already-positive state** is a legal, unhelpful move (red, Undo), never an error.
- **Phones are secondary;** every drag has a tap alternative.
