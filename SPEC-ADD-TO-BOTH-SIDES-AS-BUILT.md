## 1. What the card is

**Add to both sides** is the first card of the new moves-based **Solve it**. A student solves `x + a = b` or `x − a = b` (coefficient of x is always 1) by **adding the opposite of the number next to x to both sides**, then combining the groups until x stands alone. It teaches the idea that *the = sign is a border: both sides must hold the same amount*, so whatever you do to one side you do to the other.

- **Example:** `x − 5 = −2`. The student adds **+5** to the left, adds **+5** to the right, presses Go, combines the −5 and +5 on the left (they cancel), combines −2 and +5 on the right (a Battle that leaves +3), and types **3**.
- **What they type:** one whole number, on the integer pad (digits and ±). Never x, never an expression.
- **No streaks.** The only thing counted is **I'm stuck** clicks and **Teach me** uses, per level.
- **Where it lives:** the card is **hidden** for now. It is reachable by a link (`…/AlgebraMat/?pack=add-both&level=1`, levels 1–6, optional `&seed=7` for the same five problems again). It is **not** on the Solve it menu, does **not** save progress, and does **not** send anything to the class sheet yet. Save codes, progress migration from the old Solve it cards, the diagnostic and the menu entry are the next build.
- **Devices:** Chromebooks and laptops first (many are touch), phones second (every target ≥ 44 px, nothing wider than 390 px, every drag has a tap alternative).

## 2. Vocabulary (reuse; don't invent)

box, counters, border (the = sign), Party! (same signs add up), Battle! (opposite signs fight, the bigger side wins), cancel the pairs, **add the opposite** (never "take away from both sides"), balance, check. New on this card: **Trade places** (the two sides swap; never "flip", which is reserved for signs) and **Cross the border** (a number crosses the = and switches teams).

## 3. Levels (six; five problems each; no repeated answers within a level)

All problems are generated by code and verified by substituting the answer. Every |number| ≤ 15 (a ≤ 14); b is never 0; the answer is never 0. "Party" here means the added opposite lands on a same-sign number on the right; "Battle" means it lands on an opposite-sign number.

| Level | Forms | What's new | Working surface |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | `x + a = b`, a 1–9, b −9…12 | Add the opposite (−a). 3 Party (negative answers), 2 Battle. | Counters, **built by the student** from a tray |
| **L2** | `x − a = b`, a 2–9, b −9…12 | Adding a **positive** to cancel a negative. At least 2 Battles with negative b. | Counters, built by the student |
| **L3** | both forms mixed | Choosing the right opposite unprompted. At least 2 of each form, 2 negative and 2 positive answers. | Counters, **pre-built**, symbols shown |
| **L4** | x on the right (`7 = x + 3`) plus the L3 forms: 3 right, 2 left | **Trade places** appears (with its own tutorial). | **Symbols**; counters on request |
| **L5** | all four forms, a up to 14, b −15…15 | Bigger numbers. | Symbols |
| **L6** | x on the left only, as L5 | **Cross the border** appears (with its own tutorial). | Symbols |

Moves available: L1–L3 add; L4–L5 add + trade; L6 add + trade + cross.

Sample problems (all checked): L1 `x+3=−6`→−9, `x+5=2`→−3, `x+6=10`→4 · L2 `x−5=−2`→3, `x−4=3`→7, `x−2=−9`→−7 · L3 `x+8=3`→−5, `x−7=−4`→3 · L4 `7=x+3`→4, `5=x+9`→−4 · L5 `x+12=−3`→−15, `−6=x−9`→3 · L6 `x−4=−9`→−5, `x+8=1`→−7.

**[not built]** The original design had a combined one-tap "Add to both sides" button at L5 (type the amount, one action). Right now every level uses the two-step path below. **[not built]** The "convention nudge" for `4 = x` (a quiet line and a pulsing Trade places button): not there; a solved `4 = x` is simply credited when typed.

## 4. The screen, top to bottom (desktop; on a phone the right-hand panel moves under the board)

1. **Header and steps bar:** "Add to both sides · Level n", five dots for the five problems, and a steps bar: *Tutorial* (only while one is on), *Build it* (L1–L2 only), *Get x alone*.
2. **The balance (top left).** A big word, **Balanced ✓** (green) or **Unbalanced ✗ the right side is heavier by 9** (red), above a **seesaw**: a thick beam on a triangle pivot, with a **dashed vertical line** (the border) running up and down through the pivot. The beam **tips** (up to 8°, eased, about 0.65 s) toward the heavier side, and **the models stand on the beam**: the x box and the groups of counters (or, from L4, number tokens) sit on the left and right halves, so what is added to a side shows up on that side of the balance. The tilt comes only from the moves the student has made; it never reveals x.
3. **Add buttons under each side**, only when a number is in the blank: **"Add −9 here"** under the left half and under the right half (see §5). A side that has had its share **fades**; both fade once both have it.
4. **The equation**, under the balance, with the dashed border through the `=`. While moves happen it is live: `x + 9 + −9 = −1 + −9`.
5. **Undo (red, left) and Go ▶ / Continue ▸ (green, right)**, directly under the equation. Go only appears during an add or cross move and is enabled only when the two sides got the same amount.
6. **The counter tray** (a row of big buttons: **+** and **−**; at L1–L2 building also **□**), shown only when it is needed (building, or adding with counters).
7. **The side panel, "Balancing moves"** (right of the board; under it on a phone): a two-column grid, **each move paired with the goals it works toward** (see §8): `[Add ___ to both sides]` beside two checkboxes *Fewer terms* and *x alone*; `[Trade places]` beside *x on the left* (L4+); `[Cross the border]` (L6). Below it a quiet **Replay tutorial** button.
8. **The message line** (large, bold, under the board): one sentence that names what just happened and what to do next.
9. **Bottom controls:** a bold typing line (**x = [   ]**, or **Amount to add: [   ]**, or **How many are left? [   ]**, with a boxed value and a blinking underline while empty), then **Start over** (only after three red moves), **I'm stuck**, **Teach me step-by-step**, a **?** tour button, **Check ✓**, and the **number pad** (± 1–9 0 ⌫).

Because the board, tray and panel together are taller than a desktop window, the page scrolls a little to reach the pad. On a phone the board and tray stay in view together; the pad is a scroll below.

## 5. How a problem plays

### 5a. Building the equation (L1–L2 only)
The board starts **empty** ("Build the equation", "drop here" on each half). The student **drags the □, the + and the − up from the tray onto the beam** (or taps a piece, then taps a side). Rules: the □ only goes on the side where x lives (otherwise "The □ goes on the left in this equation"); a side's counters are **one sign only** (a group is all + or all −); tapping a placed piece takes one back off; nine counters take nine drops. When the board matches the printed equation shown under the balance, it moves on ("That is the equation. Now choose a move.").

### 5b. One move: add the same to both sides (two actions, then Go)
1. Tap **Add ___ to both sides**. The blank shows a **blinking cursor**; nothing else appears yet.
2. **Type a number** with the pad (± toggles sign), or **drag a number off the board up into the blank** (it copies the number; the student still decides the sign). A message asks "Is that what you need to add to cancel?"
3. Now that a number is in the blank, three things appear: the **"Add −9 here"** buttons under each half; the **drop areas** on the beam; and, in counter mode, the **tray** (+ and −).
4. **Add it to one side** (tap that side's button, tap the drop area, or **drag a + or − counter onto the side**). The counter's sign is checked against the blank ("The blank says −9, so it takes a − counter"). One drag/tap adds the **whole amount as one group**. The beam tips and the word says "Unbalanced: the right side is heavier by 9".
5. **Add the same to the other side** (the amount stays filled in). The beam levels: "Balanced ✓". Adding to the same side twice is refused.
6. **Go ▶** commits (enabled only when balanced).

### 5c. Simplifying: combine the groups (after Go)
While groups remain that can be combined, the student must do this before another move (the panel's moves are **faded**, a pink note says "Simplify first: tap two groups on a side to combine them", and the engine refuses a new move):
1. **Tap two groups on the same side** (tapping across the border is refused).
2. The message asks **"Party or Battle?"** The student taps **Party!** or **Battle!** (wrong pick: "Look at the signs…").
3. They **type how many are left** (and which team: a negative sign for negative). **Cross off for me** is an extra scaffold (offered after choosing Battle) that strikes the cancelling pairs, but the student still types the count. **Never mind** abandons.
4. Only then do the two groups merge. Repeat until nothing can combine; **then** the move is judged by the lights (§8).
5. When x is alone on its side and the other side is one number, the message says "x is alone: x = −10. Do you know x? Type it."

### 5d. Finishing
Type the answer and press **Check ✓**. Right: "Yes! x = 3, and it balances." and the next problem follows after a short pause. Wrong: a message that **names the slip** (§9).

### 5e. Trade places (L4+) and Cross the border (L6)
- **Trade places:** one tap. The two sides **swap around the = sign** (one travels up and over, the other down and over, about 0.9 s; nothing is mirrored; every term keeps its sign). Always allowed, always neutral; the message says "Same equation, new view."
- **Cross the border (L6):** choose it, tap a number: it jumps to the other side **keeping its sign** ("it did not switch teams: it is not balanced", the beam tips). Tap it again to **switch teams** (sign flips): balanced; **Go**.

### 5f. Undo and Start over
**Undo** (red, under the equation) always reverses one whole move (both actions together). **Start over** appears only after three red moves in a row.

## 6. Help: I'm stuck, Teach me, the wrong-move ladder, "Do you know x?"

Principle: supports are things the student **does**, never a screen they only watch; every one ends back at the pad.

- **I'm stuck**, one rung per click (+1 stuck count each, counts are per level): **Rung 1** turns counters on and highlights the number next to x ("Which number would cancel the +3? Tap it, then add its opposite to both sides. Do you know x now?"). **Rung 2** opens "Add ___ to both sides" ("Choose it and pick an amount"). **Rung 3** pre-fills the correct amount ("−3 cancels the +3. Add it to both sides yourself.").
- **Teach me step-by-step** (counts as a Teach me use): a **forced path**. Counters on, the move menu open, **only the correct amount is accepted** ("In this walkthrough the move is −3"). The student still does every add, every combine and types the answer.
- **Wrong-move ladder** (a committed move whose "Fewer terms" light is **red**): **first red** → the message says it made things worse, **Undo** is available, "try the opposite of +3"; **second red in a row** → counters open (no stuck count added); **third red** → **Start over** appears. The menu is never narrowed to the right move.
- **"Do you know x?"** is always a prompt away: mental math is welcome and never penalized.
- **Early answer** (typing an answer before x is alone): **if correct**, "Yes!", then two required steps: (1) a **check by dragging**: a chip with the typed number appears in the tray; the student **drags it onto the x** (or taps the x) and taps **Next** through the order of operations (`(3) − 5` → `3 − 5 = −2` → `−2 = −2 Balanced ✓`); (2) they do the **first good move** (add the opposite to both sides) before the problem counts. **If wrong**, the same drag-check shows **Unbalanced ✗** and the step that failed, with the slip message; it is discovery, not a stuck click.

## 7. Tutorials (a new move gets one; it plays **once per student per device**)

Karl's rule: a tutorial **justifies the new move's logic and matters, but is never a barrier that bogs students down.**
- **First time:** it plays and **cannot be skipped** (Karl found it quick). It starts on the first problem of the first level where the move appears: *Adding to both sides* (L1 or whatever level a student first opens), *Trading places* (L4), *Crossing the border* (L6). Completion is remembered in the browser (`mat.tutorials.v1`; a new device sees it once more).
- **After that:** only by choice, with **Replay tutorial** (side panel). A replay can be left at any moment with **Back to my problem**, and the student's problem is exactly as they left it.
- Tutorials never count as help (the stuck, Teach me and ? buttons are disabled while one is on).

**Adding to both sides**, in three parts:
1. **Numbers only**, `4 + 1 = 2 + 3`, with the numbers drawn as **groups of counters standing on the seesaw** and a **big readout** (`5 = 5 Balanced ✓`) with space above the balance. The student chooses "Add ___ to both sides"; the blank is **pre-filled with +3**, then (on the second move) **−2**; they add it to one side (`8 ≠ 5 Unbalanced ✗`, the beam tips) and the other (`8 = 8 ✓`), and press **Go** (the sides tidy to one number each). They **must try one positive and one negative** before **Continue** appears. Amount 0 is never accepted.
2. **Quiz, 3 questions.** A title ("Quick quiz 1 of 3"), the equation and readout, then a **bold question in its own spot** ("What would happen if we added 3 to one side only? Does it keep the balance, or upset it?") with the two answer buttons **right under it** (*Keeps the balance* / *Upsets the balance*). The three items: 3 to one side only (upsets); 3 to both sides (keeps); 3 to the left and 2 to the right (upsets). A wrong answer shows **Before / After** equations with readouts under the buttons, and the student retries that item.
3. **Counters with a box**, then **symbols only**, both on the real card on `x + 2 = 5` in the forced path (only −2 accepted), ending with the typed answer 3 and a **Continue**.

**Trading places:** `5 = 2 + 3` → **Trade places** → `2 + 3 = 5` (animated), then `7 = 4 + 3`; quiz of 2 (trading keeps; changing the sign of one term upsets).
**Crossing the border:** `4 + 1 = 2 + 3`; tap a number to cross (`4 ≠ 6 ✗`), tap it to switch teams (`4 = 4 ✓`), **Go**; **Change a sign** (one sign upsets) and **Change every sign** (keeps); quiz of 2 (crossing and not switching: upsets; crossing and switching: keeps).

## 8. The lights (now checkboxes, paired with moves)

Shown in the side panel next to the move that works toward them; judged **only after a move is fully committed and combined** (never mid-move). Each is a small square: **green ✓** (earned), **red ✗**, **grey –** (neutral), or a **dashed empty** box (not yet available).

| Goal | Beside | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Fewer terms** | Add ___ to both sides | **Green** if the number of terms went down; **neutral** if unchanged; **red** if terms went **up or the number next to x got bigger in size** (e.g. adding +3 to `x + 3 = −6` makes `x + 6 = −3`). |
| **x alone** | Add ___ to both sides | Greyed out until a move has earned "Fewer terms" green in this problem; then **green** when x is the only term on its side, else neutral. Never red. |
| **x on the left** | Trade places (L4+) | Greyed out until "x alone" is green; then **green** if x is on the left, **neutral** if on the right. Never red. |

No size or fraction lights on this card (those come with later cards).

## 9. What a wrong answer looks like (typed-answer slips)

Each wrong typed answer is **tagged** by which mistake produces exactly that number, then answered with a message built from the equation (`con` = the number next to x, `opp` = its opposite). Examples are checked by code.

| Slip | Example → wrong answer | Message (shape) |
|---|---|---|
| **Added the same number, not its opposite** | `x+3=−6` → −3 | "Put −3 back in for x and it doesn't balance. The +3 next to x needs its opposite to cancel: add −3 to both sides, not +3." (x-on-the-right form: "The +3 changed sides but stayed +3…") |
| **Ignored the number next to x** | `x+3=−6` → −6 | "…What's still hiding next to x? The +3 is still there." |
| **Right size, wrong team / sign dropped** | `x+4=−2` → 6; `x+5=2` → 3 | Party: "Right size, wrong team: check the sign." Battle: "Battle! −5 and +2 fight, and the bigger team wins: which team has more?" |
| **Added the sizes of opposite numbers** (a Battle treated as a Party; only on Battles) | `x−5=−2` → 7 | "+5 and −2 are opposites, so it's a Battle, not a Party: take the bigger side's sign." |
| **Anything else** | | "Cancel the +3 with its opposite, −3, on both sides." |

**Combining slips** (when the student types how many are left): adding the sizes of opposite groups ("The pairs cancel. Count what is left."), the right size on the wrong team ("Which team has more?"), or anything else ("Not quite. Try again, or tap Cross off to see the pairs cancel.").

**Move slips** (judged by the lights or the balance): adding the **same sign** as the number next to x (red; "try the opposite"); a **partly cancelling** amount (neutral; "The +3 is still there"); adding to **one side only** (the beam stays tipped, Go stays disabled: "Both sides must hold the same amount"); wrong **Party/Battle** pick; a **counter of the wrong sign** dragged in; crossing without switching teams.

## 10. Edge cases and rules the build already enforces

Answer never 0, b never 0, no repeated answers or equations in a level; all numbers |n| ≤ 15 and at most 36 counters a side; amount 0 is never accepted; a group of counters is one sign; Undo reverses both actions of a move together; while simplifying no new move can start; the tilt is computed only from the student's moves; Trade places on a solved equation is allowed and neutral; tutorial numbers are chosen so nothing balances by accident; text on the Mat can't be selected by dragging.

## 11. Things I think are weak or unsettled (please attack these)

1. **Is the screen too much?** Balance word, seesaw with models, side add buttons, equation, Undo/Go, tray, panel with six checkboxes, message line, typing line, help buttons and pad. It scrolls on a desktop. What should go, move, or only appear when needed?
2. **The two-action move** (add to one side, then the other) is there so students *feel* the tilt. After a dozen problems is it just busywork? The original design added a one-tap combined button at L5 **[not built]**: is that the right place, or is there a better "graduation"?
3. **Combining is a three-step ritual** (tap two groups, say Party/Battle, type the count). It teaches the vocabulary but is slow on a Chromebook, and a problem needs two or three combines. Where should it relax (maybe at L4+ once symbols are on)?
4. **The seesaw tilts by what the student added, not by real weights.** Add +3 to the left and the left side drops; that is the point. But x's value is hidden, so the beam can only ever show the *difference between the two adds*, never the equation's real weight. Is that honest enough, or will a student read the beam as "the equation is balanced because x equals something"?
5. **Counter drag adds a whole amount as one group** (one drag = +9). Dragging nine separate counters would be more concrete but slow. Is one drag the right grain at L1–L2?
6. **The "Fewer terms" red rule** (terms up, or the coefficient next to x grows) can feel punishing right after an honest mistake. Is red needed, or would neutral plus a tip be kinder?
7. **The tutorial is unskippable the first time.** It is short, but a strong student who already knows this will still do it. Right trade-off? And the quiz is only three yes/no items: does it check understanding, or can it be guessed (a coin flip gets a retry)?
8. **Trade places at L4 and Cross the border at L6** each get a numbers-only tutorial and then are "optional but useful." Is there enough reason for a student to *choose* them, or will they never use them?
9. **Stuck and Teach me data** is intended for me (counts per level, anonymous). I also don't track undo counts, red moves, or which moves a student actually uses. Is that the right amount of teacher data?
10. **No streaks, no celebration beyond "Yes!".** Is there something that rewards a clean problem without turning it into a speed game?
